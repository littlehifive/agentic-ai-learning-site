24.2.1 & 24.2.3 Centralized and hierarchical

A lead agent delegates to the right workers

The most common starting shape. One supervisor holds the big picture, breaks the task into pieces, hands each piece to the worker best suited for it, and stitches the results back together. Everything flows through the hub.

Supervisor (hub and spoke)

One manager routes each sub-task, gives each worker just the context it needs, gathers the outputs, and handles failures by retrying or re-routing.

Good because control is simple and every decision sits in one place, so it is easy to debug. Risky because the manager is a single point of failure and a bottleneck.

Hierarchical (a tree of managers)

The same idea, but taller. A top orchestrator delegates to domain sub-managers, who delegate to their own workers. Authority flows down; results flow up; problems escalate.

Good because each level only tracks its own slice, so it scales to many agents without one mind holding everything.

Think of it like a company. A supervisor is a manager with a small team. A hierarchy is a CEO who sets strategy, VPs who turn it into plans, and contributors who do the work. Scale without losing accountability.

In plain terms

SupervisorA lead agent that splits the task, assigns workers, and combines their results.
Delegation chainThe path tasks travel down the tree and results travel back up.
EscalationA worker handing a problem it cannot solve back up to its manager.
