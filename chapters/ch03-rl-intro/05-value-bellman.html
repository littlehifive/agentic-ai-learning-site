3.2 Core concepts: value, Q, and Bellman

How good is this spot? Value answers that

To choose well, the agent needs to know what a situation is worth, not just the reward right now but all the reward it can expect from here on. These value estimates are the agent's compass.

Value, V(s)

Worth of a state

Total reward you can expect starting from this situation and playing on.

Q-value, Q(s, a)

Worth of a move

Total reward from taking one specific action here, then continuing.

Advantage, A(s, a)

Better than average?

How much this action beats the typical action from this state: Q minus V.

The Bellman equation ties it together: the value of where you are equals the reward you get next plus the (discounted) value of where you land. Value is defined in terms of itself, one step at a time.

In plain terms

ReturnThe full sum of rewards from now on, with later rewards shrunk by the discount factor.
Optimal policyOnce you know the best Q-value for every move, the best policy is simple: in each state, pick the action with the highest Q.
