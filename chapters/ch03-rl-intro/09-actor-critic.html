3.7 Actor-critic methods

Two roles: one acts, one keeps score

REINFORCE waits for a full run before it knows how good a move was, which makes its feedback jumpy. Actor-critic adds a second network, the critic, that learns to judge each move on the spot. The actor still chooses; the critic supplies a steady, low-noise verdict to learn from.

The actor

Picks the action

The policy. Proposes what to do, and gets tuned toward moves the critic rates above average.

⇄ act, then judge

The critic

Rates the action

A learned value function. Estimates how good a move was without waiting for the episode to finish, using the TD error.

Think of it like a singer and a vocal coach. The singer performs, the coach gives instant feedback after each phrase rather than waiting for the concert to end, and the singer adjusts the very next line. Faster, calmer learning than waiting for the final applause.

In plain terms

Advantage signalThe critic tells the actor not just "good or bad" but "how much better than average", which is exactly what makes the update efficient.
Lower variancePer-step critic feedback is far less noisy than one reward at the end, so training is steadier. PPO, used to align LLMs, is an actor-critic method.
