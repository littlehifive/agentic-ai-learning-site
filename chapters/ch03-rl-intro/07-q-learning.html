3.5 Q-learning

Keep a scorecard of every move in every spot

Q-learning fills in a table of Q-values, one score per state-and-action pair. After each step it nudges the score it just used toward the reward it got plus the value of the best move available next. Over time the table converges, and acting well becomes "read the row, pick the highest number".

The gold cell is the best move for that state. The policy is just "pick the highest Q in your row".

Think of it like a well-worn restaurant guide. Each entry rates a dish at a place; you keep updating ratings as you eat, and you order by reading off the highest score. It learns the best rating even while you sometimes try a random dish to explore.

In plain terms

Off-policyIt learns the best moves even from random or old experience, because the update always aims at the best next action, not the one actually taken.
Why not for LLMsA language model has tens of thousands of actions (the vocabulary) at every step. Scoring them all per token is intractable, so LLMs use policy methods instead.
