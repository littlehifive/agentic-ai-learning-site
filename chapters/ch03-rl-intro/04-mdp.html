3.1 The Markov Decision Process (MDP)

The world, written down as five moving parts

An MDP is the standard way to describe any RL problem. It names the situations the agent can be in, the moves it can make, how the world reacts, the reward it pays, and how much the future counts. The Markov property keeps it simple: the next state depends only on where you are now, not on the whole history of how you got there.

S

States

Every situation the world can be in.

A

Actions

Every move available to the agent.

P

Transitions

Odds of landing in each next state after a move.

R

Reward

The number paid out for a transition.

γ

Discount

How much a future reward is worth today, from 0 to 1.

Think of it like a board game. The squares are the states, the legal moves are the actions, the dice decide where you land, the points are the reward, and the discount says a point now beats a point ten turns from now.

In plain terms

Markov propertyThe future depends only on the current state, not the path that led there. This is what makes the maths tractable.
Discount factorA number near 1 means the agent is patient and plans far ahead; near 0 means it only chases immediate reward.
