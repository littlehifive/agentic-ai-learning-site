3.6 Policy gradient methods: REINFORCE

Skip the table. Tune the behaviour directly

Instead of scoring every action and reading off the best, policy gradients adjust the policy itself. Run it, see how the whole attempt scored, then make the good attempts more likely and the bad ones less likely. REINFORCE is the simplest version of this idea.

Good run Reward came out high

Push up the probability of all the actions taken. Do more of that.

Bad run Reward came out low

Push down the probability of those actions. Do less of that.

Think of it like coaching by results. The coach does not grade each pass; the team plays a full match, and afterwards the moves from winning games get practised more, the moves from losses less. It is supervised learning where the labels are your own actions, weighted by how well they paid off.

In plain terms

Policy gradientThe direction to shift the policy so that higher-reward actions become more probable.
BaselineSubtract the average value first, so you reward "better than usual" rather than just "positive". Same target, far less noise.
The catchREINFORCE is very noisy: one full run per update, used once then thrown away. That high variance is what the next methods fix.
