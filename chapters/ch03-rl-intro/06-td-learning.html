3.4 Temporal-difference (TD) learning

Learn from surprise, without waiting for the end

You do not need to finish the whole trip to learn. The moment reality differs from your guess, you can update. TD learning measures that gap, the TD error, and nudges its estimate toward the new, better guess at every single step.

Your prediction

30 min

You set off expecting the drive home to take half an hour.

→

Reality hits

45 min

Ten minutes in you meet roadworks; the GPS now says 35 left, so 45 total.

→

The TD error

+15 min

The new estimate beats the old by 15 minutes. That surprise is the learning signal.

Read it like a positive surprise means a state turned out better than expected, so raise its value and seek it out. A negative surprise means it was worse than expected, so lower its value and avoid it. Drive the surprise toward zero and the estimates have converged.

In plain terms

BootstrappingUpdating a guess using another guess (the value of the next state), instead of waiting for the final outcome.
TD vs Monte CarloMonte Carlo waits for the full episode: unbiased but jumpy. TD updates each step: a little biased but far steadier.
