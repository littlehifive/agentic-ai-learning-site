12.5.1 STaR: Self-Taught Reasoner

The model learns from its own correct work

STaR needs no human grader and no separate reward model. The insight is simple: if the model can solve a problem correctly even occasionally, those wins are free training data. Keep the good attempts, train on them, and the next round produces more good attempts.

Step 1

Generate

Have the model work through a batch of problems, showing its reasoning.

→

Step 2

Filter

Keep only the attempts that reached the correct answer. Throw out the rest.

→

Step 3

Fine-tune

Train the model on those successful reasoning traces.

↻ Repeat. Each round the improved model solves more, so the next batch of training data gets better.

Think of it like a student keeping only the homework they got right and re-studying it. Their hit rate climbs each pass, typically from about 30 percent up to 70 to 90 percent over a few rounds.

In plain terms

BootstrapImproving by pulling on your own successes, with no outside teacher.
RationalizationSTaR's twist: for problems it failed, show it the answer and let it reason backward to learn the path.
