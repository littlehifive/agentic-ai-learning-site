13.1.3 Test-time compute scaling laws

Let it think longer, and it does measurably better

The central finding behind reasoning models: accuracy climbs predictably as you let the model spend more "thinking" tokens at answer time. A small model given a big thinking budget can rival a much larger model that answers in one shot.

Short thinking low More thinking higher Long thinking highest
Think of it like a student in an exam. Given five minutes they rush and slip up; given an hour to work carefully and double-check, the same student scores far higher. The extra time is the extra compute.

In plain terms

Test-time computeWork done when answering, not when training. Roughly, how many tokens the model is allowed to think for.
Thinking budgetA dial for how long it may deliberate. Bigger budget, harder problems within reach, but slower replies.
The tradeYou can buy accuracy with training compute up front, or with thinking compute at answer time. They are roughly interchangeable.
