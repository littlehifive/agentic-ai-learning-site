13.2.2 Self-consistency, in depth

Why many tries beat one good try

Self-consistency is the cheapest way to spend more compute for more accuracy. If each chain is even somewhat likely to be right, and wrong answers spread out while right answers pile up, then counting votes across more chains keeps pushing accuracy up.

What it does

  • Generate several reasoning chains for one question, with a little randomness so they differ.
  • Read off the final answer from each chain.
  • Return whichever answer the most chains agreed on.

Why it helps

  • Errors are scattered and rarely agree; correct reasoning concentrates on one answer.
  • More chains means more accuracy, with gains tapering off after a few dozen.
  • No extra training and no special tooling, just ask more times and tally.
Think of it like asking a roomful of people to each work the problem alone, then going with the answer most of them reached. The crowd converges on the truth.

In plain terms

Self-consistencySample several reasoning paths, then take the majority answer.
