13.1 Motivation and background

The same model, but now it shows its working

A standard model maps a question straight to an answer. A reasoning model inserts a middle stage: a private stretch of "thinking" where it lays out steps, tries an approach, notices mistakes, and only then commits to a final reply.

Question

The prompt

A hard problem comes in: a maths puzzle, a piece of code, a proof to complete.

→

New stage

Thinking out loud

The model writes a chain of reasoning steps, explores options, and self-corrects ("Wait, let me reconsider").

→

Answer

The final reply

Only the checked conclusion is returned. The thinking did the hard work of getting there.

Think of it like the difference between shouting out the first answer that pops into your head and working it through on scratch paper. The scratch paper is where most problems are actually solved.

In plain terms

Reasoning stepOne link in the working, such as a single line of a calculation or a sub-conclusion.
Self-correctionThe model noticing its own error part-way through and changing course, instead of pressing on.
