13.2.1 Chain-of-thought

Chain-of-thought: solve it one step at a time

Instead of jumping to an answer, the model writes out the intermediate steps, like showing your working in maths. Breaking a hard problem into small pieces lets each piece be easy, and the right answer falls out at the end.

Straight to the answer

"A shop has 23 apples, uses 20, buys 6 more. How many now?"

"9." (a quick guess, easy to get wrong)

With a chain of thought

"A shop has 23 apples, uses 20, buys 6 more. How many now?"

Start with 23.
Use 20, so 23 − 20 = 3 left.
Buy 6 more, so 3 + 6 = 9.

"9." (and now you can see why)

Think of it like talking yourself through a recipe out loud rather than trying to remember the whole dish at once. Each step is small, and the next step only needs the one before it.

In plain terms

Chain-of-thought (CoT)Writing out the reasoning steps between question and answer, not just the answer.
The catchA single chain is fragile. One wrong early step and everything after it is built on sand, with no way to recover.
