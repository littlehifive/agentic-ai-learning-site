13.2.2 Self-consistency

One quick guess, or many tries and a vote?

Click a tab to switch the strategy

"42"1 vote

A single reasoning chain gives a single answer. If that one chain slips early, you are simply stuck with a wrong answer and no way to tell.

"24"3 votes
"42"1 vote
"18"1 vote

Five independent chains, then the most common answer wins. Correct chains tend to agree on one value; wrong chains scatter. The majority, "24", is the answer.

Mistakes are random and disagree with each other; correct reasoning keeps landing on the same place. Counting votes turns several shaky tries into one reliable answer.

In plain terms

SamplingAsking the model the same question several times so it produces several different reasoning paths.
Majority voteReturning the answer that the most paths agreed on.
