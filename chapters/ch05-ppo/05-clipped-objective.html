5.2 The clipped objective

A guardrail caps how far one update can move

PPO measures how much more or less likely the new model is to make each choice, compared with the model that produced the answer. It will reward improvement, but only up to a fixed limit, usually about twenty percent. Past that limit, extra change earns nothing, so there is no reason to lurch.

change limited to within 1 minus ε and 1 plus ε  ·  typical ε = 0.2

When a choice was good

Make it more likely, but stop at the cap. The lesson is "do not get greedy on one good example." A single lucky answer should not let the model bet everything on that one pattern.

When a choice was bad

Make it less likely, but stop at the cap. The lesson is "do not forget too aggressively on one bad example." One mistake should not erase a habit that is usually fine.

Think of it like a guardrail on a mountain road. You can steer freely within the lane, but the rail sets a hard limit on how far any single swerve can take you, so one bad reflex can never send the car over the edge.

In plain terms

Probability ratioHow much more or less likely the new model is to make a choice than the old one.
ClipCut off any change beyond the allowed band so extra movement gives no extra credit.
Trust regionThe small zone around the current model where updates are considered safe.
