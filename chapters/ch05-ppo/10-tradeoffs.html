Reading the tradeoffs

Powerful and stable, but heavy to run

PPO became the default for aligning models to human preferences because it is reliable. That reliability comes at a real cost in machinery and tuning, which is exactly what later methods try to trim.

What it buys you

  • Stable training: one bad batch cannot wreck the model.
  • Steady, monotonic improvement step after step.
  • Works from any reward signal, including a learned scorer.
  • Simple first-order math, easy to scale across many machines.

What it costs you

  • Four models in memory at once, so it is resource-hungry.
  • Constant fresh generation, often most of the wall-clock time.
  • Several knobs that all interact and need careful tuning.
  • A separate reward model to train and maintain.
Reach for PPO when you need robust learning from a flexible reward and can afford the compute. When the cost bites, the next chapters trim it: DPO drops the reward model and the loop, and GRPO drops the value model.
