7.1 What you gain by removing it

One fewer model means cheaper and simpler

Because the group does the critic's job, that whole second network disappears. The win is both practical and, surprisingly, often a quality gain too.

Lighter and easier to run

The critic was about as large as the model itself. Dropping it frees up a large slice of memory and removes an entire training process to babysit.

That makes GRPO the practical choice for very large models, where finding room for two networks at once is the binding constraint.

Often more accurate, not less

A measured average from real answers is honest by construction. A half-trained critic can simply be wrong, and a wrong bar sends the update the wrong way.

So replacing a shaky guess with a real measurement frequently makes learning more stable, not just cheaper.

In plain terms

The tradeYou pay to generate several answers per prompt, but you save the cost of training and storing a whole second network.
Net effectLess memory, half the engineering, and a baseline that cannot be miscalibrated the way a learned critic can.
