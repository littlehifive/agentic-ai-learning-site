11.12 Cost Analysis and Cloud Deployment

Every GPU is a choice about money and speed

GPUs are expensive and finite, so allocating them is a budget exercise. A full feedback run on a 70-billion-parameter model can take dozens of GPUs for days. Two questions dominate: how to split them between writing and training, and which chips to rent.

How many on each side

Give too many to the writing cluster and the trainer starves; too few and answers trickle in. Because the phases overlap, the goal is to keep both clusters busy and neither waiting on the other.

Which chips, at what price

Top-end chips train faster but cost more. Cheaper chips can be fine for the memory-bound writing phase. Rented spare capacity is far cheaper but can vanish mid-run, so it is only safe with solid backups.

Think of it like staffing a restaurant: you balance prep cooks against line cooks so neither stands idle, and you decide where to spend on star chefs versus where a capable line cook does the job just as well.
