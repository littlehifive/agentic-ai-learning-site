11.3 The Generation Bottleneck

Writing answers and learning from them stress different hardware

Each training round has two very different phases. First the model writes practice answers, then it learns from the feedback. They do not strain the GPU the same way, which is the root of many design choices later in the chapter.

Writing answers is memory-bound

To produce each word, the GPU must read the entire model from memory but does very little math. It spends almost all its time waiting on memory, leaving its calculating power nearly idle.

Learning is compute-bound

Updating the model is the opposite: a heavy burst of math that keeps the calculating cores fully busy and barely touches memory speed. The bottleneck is raw compute.

Think of it like a chef who spends most of his time walking to the pantry for one ingredient at a time (writing answers), versus the same chef in a furious burst of chopping at the counter (learning). One job is limited by the trips, the other by the knife work.

In plain terms

Memory-boundLimited by how fast data can be read from memory, not by calculating speed.
Compute-boundLimited by raw calculating power, with memory speed to spare.
Why it mattersNo single setup is best for both, which pushes teams to handle the two phases separately.
