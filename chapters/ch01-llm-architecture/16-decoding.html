1.12 Decoding methods

How the model turns scores into an actual word

Click a prompt to switch the example

"4"96%
"four"2%
"five"1%

A confident moment: top-p keeps essentially just "4", while a fixed top-k would needlessly keep dozens of near-zero options.

"pizza"11%
"sushi"10%
"pasta"9%

An open moment: top-p widens to cover the many good options, while a small fixed top-k would cut most of them off.

Top-p adapts to how sure the model is: it shrinks when the model is confident and grows when it is not. Top-k always keeps the same fixed number of options.

In plain terms

GreedyAlways take the single highest-scoring word: safe for facts, but repetitive.
TemperatureA creativity dial: low makes the model cautious, high makes it adventurous.
