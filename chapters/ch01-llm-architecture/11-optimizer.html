1.5 Optimization: how a model learns

Training nudges billions of dials toward fewer mistakes

Learning means repeatedly checking how wrong the model is, then adjusting its internal numbers a little to do better next time. Two choices dominate how well this goes.

The optimizer: AdamW

The optimizer decides how big each adjustment should be. AdamW gives every dial its own adaptive step size and gently shrinks unused dials toward zero. It is the default for training language models because it is stable across very different layers.

The learning rate: the key knob

The learning rate sets the overall step size. Too high and training blows up; too low and it crawls. It is the single most important setting, so it is the first thing practitioners tune.

Think of it like walking downhill in fog to find the lowest point. The learning rate is your step size: big strides risk overshooting the valley, tiny steps take forever.

In plain terms

Gradient descentRepeatedly step in the direction that reduces error, a little at a time.
Weight decayA gentle pull on the dials toward zero, which discourages overcomplicated solutions.
