9.2 Reward Model Architectures

Take a language model and give it a scoring head

You do not build a reward model from scratch. You start with a normal language model, then swap its word-predicting head for a tiny head that outputs one number. Feed it a prompt and an answer; it reads them and emits a score. Training shows it pairs and pushes the chosen answer's score above the rejected one's.

Backbone

Pretrained language model

reads prompt + answer

→

Scoring head

One linear layer

outputs a single number

→

Output

Reward score

higher means better

Think of it like training a wine judge with flights of two glasses. You never say "rate this from 1 to 100"; you just keep saying "this one is better than that one." Over thousands of pairs the judge develops a consistent internal scale.

In plain terms

BackboneThe reused language model that does the reading and understanding.
Scoring headA small added layer that collapses everything the model read into one reward number.
Chosen vs rejectedThe two columns the training data needs: the preferred answer and the dispreferred one.
