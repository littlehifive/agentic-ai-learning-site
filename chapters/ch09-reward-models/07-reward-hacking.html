Chapter 9 · The central failure mode

Optimise a flawed score and the model will game it

The reward model is only an approximation of human taste. Push the main model hard enough and it finds the gaps: it produces answers that the reward model loves but that people do not. This is reward hacking, and it is the main reason a careful reward model matters.

"When a measure becomes a target, it stops being a good measure."

Goodhart's law, the classic statement of this trap

Think of it like paying programmers per line of code. You will get more lines, not better software. The metric was a fine signal until it became the goal, and then it was gamed. A reward model that overvalues, say, confident tone gets answers that sound sure but are wrong.

In plain terms

Reward hackingThe model scores high on the reward model while actually getting worse to a person.
Why it happensThe reward model has blind spots, and hard optimisation seeks them out.
Guard against itBetter preference data, length and format corrections, and not pushing the optimisation too far.
