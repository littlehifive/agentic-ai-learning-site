9.1 Bradley-Terry Model

If people pick A more often, A should score higher

The Bradley-Terry model is the rule for converting "how often A beats B" into scores. The bigger the gap between two scores, the more confidently the higher one should win the head-to-head comparison. Training simply searches for the scores that best match the win-rates people actually gave.

Chosen 80% of the timeAnswer A
→
Higher scoreA scores well above B
A wins four times out of five, so its score pulls clearly ahead.
Think of it like chess ratings. Nobody is handed a number; ratings are inferred from who beats whom and by how often. Beat strong players regularly and your rating climbs. Bradley-Terry does the same for answers.

In plain terms

Win-rateThe share of comparisons one answer wins against another.
Score differenceOnly the gap between two scores matters, not their absolute size; a larger gap means a more lopsided preference.
TransitiveThe model assumes a clean order: if A beats B and B beats C, then A should beat C.
