9.4 Process vs Outcome Reward Models

Grade only the final answer, or grade every step

For a problem solved in several steps, you can reward in two ways. An Outcome Reward Model checks just the end result. A Process Reward Model checks the reasoning step by step. The first is cheap and sparse; the second is costly but gives feedback all the way through.

ORM

Outcome Reward Model

Looks only at the final answer and asks: right or wrong? Cheap to label and run, but it gives no hint about where a long chain of reasoning went off the rails.

PRM

Process Reward Model

Scores each reasoning step along the way. Far richer feedback, so the model learns which moves were sound, but it needs step-by-step labels that are expensive to gather.

Think of it like a maths teacher. An outcome grader marks only the boxed answer. A process grader gives partial credit line by line, so a student who set the problem up well but slipped on arithmetic still learns where they went wrong.

In plain terms

Sparse feedbackOne signal at the very end; the model must guess which steps mattered.
Dense feedbackA signal at every step, so credit lands on the exact move that helped or hurt.
