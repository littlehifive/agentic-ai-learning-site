9.3 Reward Model Training Tricks

Two fixes keep the score honest

A raw reward model has two well-known quirks. Its numbers drift to an arbitrary scale, and it quietly rewards long answers just for being long. Both are fixed with small adjustments before the score is trusted.

Reward centering

The raw scores can sit anywhere, say all clustered around 7 or around -3. That offset is meaningless and makes the next training stage jittery. Centering shifts the scores so the average is zero, so only the differences between answers carry weight.

Length-bias correction

Reward models tend to score longer answers higher even when they are no better. Left alone, the main model learns to ramble. The fix is to discount for length, so a score reflects quality rather than word count.

Think of it like grading essays. Centering is marking on a curve so the class average is fixed and only relative standing matters. Length correction is refusing to give more credit just because a paper is thicker.

In plain terms

CenteringSubtracting the average so scores hover around zero and the next stage trains smoothly.
Length biasThe tendency to reward longer text regardless of quality, corrected before the score is used.
