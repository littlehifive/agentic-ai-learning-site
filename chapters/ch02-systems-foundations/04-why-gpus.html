2.1.1 Why GPUs for Deep Learning?

Why models run on GPUs, not ordinary CPUs

A model's core work is multiplying huge grids of numbers, the same simple step repeated billions of times. A CPU does a few things very quickly one after another. A GPU does thousands of small things at once, which is exactly the shape of this work, making training 100 to 1000 times faster.

CPU

A few fast lanes

Built for speed on one task at a time, with 8 to 96 powerful cores. Great for varied, branching work, but it would crawl through billions of identical multiplications.

GPU

Thousands of small lanes

Built for volume: thousands of simple units running in parallel. Each does easy math, but together they chew through the grid multiplications that models are made of.

Think of it like shipping a million identical postcards. A CPU is one expert courier who is very fast but handles one card at a time. A GPU is a stadium full of helpers, each slower, but all stamping cards at the same moment.

In plain terms

GPUA chip with thousands of small units that do simple math all at once, ideal for the grid multiplications inside a model.
ParallelMany calculations happening at the same time instead of one after another.
ThroughputHow much total work finishes per second, the thing GPUs are built to maximize.
