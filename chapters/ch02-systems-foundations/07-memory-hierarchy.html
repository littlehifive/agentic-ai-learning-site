Bar length shows relative speed. Each step down is roughly 10x slower but 100 to 1000x larger.

A GPU does not have one kind of memory. It has a ladder. At the top sit tiny stores that the math units can read instantly. At the bottom sits the big main memory (HBM) that holds the whole model but is far slower to reach. The model only ever lives in the big slow store, so every calculation means hauling numbers up the ladder.

Think of it like cooking. Ingredients in your hand are instant, the counter is close, the fridge is a few steps, and the grocery store across town holds everything but takes ages. You win by keeping what you need close.

In plain terms

HBMThe GPU's large main memory; roomy enough for the model, but the slowest to read.
Shared memory (SRAM)A small on-chip notepad that the math units reach almost instantly.
