2.1.10 Choosing Parallelism · Distributed Training

Four ways to split the work across GPUs

If a model is too big or training is too slow for one GPU, you spread it out. There are four basic strategies, and real systems mix them. The trick is to keep the chattiest splits inside one machine, where the links are fastest.

Data parallel (copy the model)

Every GPU holds the full model and trains on different examples, then they average their lessons. Simple, and the most common starting point.

FSDP / ZeRO (shard the bookkeeping)

Like data parallel, but the bulky training bookkeeping is split across GPUs instead of copied. Each fetches a piece only when needed, so far bigger models fit.

Tensor parallel (split each layer)

A single layer is sliced across several GPUs that compute it together. Very chatty, so keep it inside one machine over NVLink.

Pipeline parallel (split into stages)

Different GPUs own different layers, like stations on an assembly line. The model's data flows through stage by stage. Can span machines.

Think of it like a big order in a kitchen. Many cooks each make full meals (data parallel), or one dish is built along an assembly line (pipeline), or one huge dish is chopped up among cooks working shoulder to shoulder (tensor).

FSDP / ZeROA memory-saving form of data parallel that splits the training bookkeeping across GPUs instead of copying it.
