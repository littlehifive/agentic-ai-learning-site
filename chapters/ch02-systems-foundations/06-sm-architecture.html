One A100 chip packs 108 of these cells

A GPU is not one big processor. It is built from many small, identical work cells, each a complete mini-processor with its own math units and scratch space. Inside each cell sit tensor cores: special units that multiply small grids of numbers in one step, about 16 times faster than ordinary math. Stack a hundred of these cells together and you get the raw power a model needs.

Think of it like a kitchen with 108 identical cook stations. Each has its own counter and tools, and a special pasta machine (the tensor core) that turns out grids of pasta far faster than chopping by hand.

In plain terms

SM (work cell)A self-contained mini-processor on the GPU; an A100 has 108 of them.
Tensor coreA unit built only for multiplying small grids of numbers, the model's most common operation.
