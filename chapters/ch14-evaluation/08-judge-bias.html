14.7.2 Position Bias Mitigation

The judge has blind spots, and they are fixable

A model judge is not neutral. It leans toward the answer shown first, toward longer replies, and toward its own style. Left unchecked these tilts can swing a verdict by ten to fifteen points. Each one has a known fix.

Common biases

Position bias: prefers whichever answer it sees first or last.
Verbosity bias: rewards longer answers even when the extra text adds nothing.
Self-enhancement bias: favours answers that match its own style or its own outputs.

How to reduce them

Swap the order: grade both A-then-B and B-then-A. If the verdict flips, call it a tie.
Reason first: make the judge explain before deciding, which curbs snap reactions to length or order.
Use a panel: several judges from different model families, then take the majority.
Think of it like a blind taste test. You hide the labels, serve the samples in random order, and ask several tasters, so no single quirk decides the winner.

In plain terms

The riskAn unchecked judge can be steered by order or length rather than true quality.
The habitRandomise position, ask for reasoning, and average across judges before you trust the score.
