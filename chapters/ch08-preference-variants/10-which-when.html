8.6 Choosing a method

Start from your constraint, not the method

Do not ask which method is best. Ask what is holding you back, then read across to the variant built to remove it. These are the questions the book walks through, in order.

What does your data look like?

  • Matched good-and-bad pairs → DPO
  • Those pairs are noisy or AI-judged → IPO
  • Only thumbs-up / down on single answers → KTO
  • No preference data, just a scorer → Best-of-N

What is your tightest limit?

  • DPO has stopped improving → Online DPO
  • Memory is the wall → ORPO or SimPO
  • Starting from a raw base model → ORPO
  • You just need a fast, solid baseline → Best-of-N
A useful habit: always measure your fancy method against plain Best-of-N on the same compute budget. If the heavy method cannot beat it, the heavy method has a bug.
