8.2 KTO · Kahneman-Tversky Optimization

Learn from a thumbs-up or a thumbs-down

The problem it fixes: DPO needs a matched pair for every prompt, a good answer and a bad one side by side. Real feedback almost never arrives that way. People just tap thumbs-up or thumbs-down on one answer. KTO learns from those single verdicts, no pairing required, so each rating stands on its own.

Think of it like a restaurant reading one-star and five-star reviews. It never needs two reviews of the same dish to know which dishes to keep. Borrowing from behavioral economics, KTO also leans harder on a complaint than on praise, because a bad answer should sting more.

In plain terms

The fixEach answer carries its own label, good or bad. No matched pairs needed.
BonusHandles lopsided data well, like ninety percent good and ten percent bad, and tolerates noisy labels.
Reach for it whenYou have live thumbs-up and thumbs-down from real traffic but no curated pairs.
