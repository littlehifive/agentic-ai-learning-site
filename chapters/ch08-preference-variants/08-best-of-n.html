8.5 Best-of-N · rejection sampling

Skip the training, just keep the best draft

The problem it fixes: all the methods above are training. Best-of-N needs none. For each prompt, generate several answers, have a scorer rate them, and serve the top one. Quality climbs without ever touching the model's weights. Those winning answers can also be saved and fine-tuned on later, which is how many real models are built.

Think of it like a writer who drafts an email five times and sends the best one. Same writer, better output, just from picking. Generate sixteen tries and quality often jumps ten to fifteen percent, sometimes matching far heavier methods.

In plain terms

The fixNo alignment training at all. Sample several answers, score them, keep the winner.
The priceYou pay at answer time: generating N answers costs roughly N times the compute.
Reach for it whenYou want a strong, dead-simple baseline, or a way to generate training data for later.
