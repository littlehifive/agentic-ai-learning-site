23.8 Security and trust in multi-agent systems

Delegation only works if you can trust the chain

When agent A asks B, and B asks C, the trust has to hold all the way down. A2A treats identity, permissions, and a record of who did what as core parts of the protocol, not extras.

Who is calling

Authentication

Each agent proves its identity with a signed token or certificate before any work begins, so no impostor can slip in.

What they may ask

Authorization

Scopes limit each caller to what it is allowed to request. A reporting agent might read data but never be granted write access.

What happened

Audit trails

Every exchange is logged with the caller, the request, and the outcome, so responsibility stays clear across a chain of handoffs.

Think of it like a secure office building. A badge proves who you are, the badge only opens certain doors, and a log records every door you went through.

In plain terms

AuthenticationProving you are who you claim to be.
AuthorizationChecking whether you are allowed to do the specific thing you asked.
