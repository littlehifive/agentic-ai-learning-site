28.13 · Method selection decision tree

Picking an alignment method: start with your data

What you have on hand decides the method. Read top to bottom and stop at the first row that matches.

What you haveUseWhy
Clean pairs of better and worse answers, limited computeDPOLearns straight from preferences, no separate scoring model needed.
Preference pairs, but the labels are noisyIPOA steadier variant that resists over-fitting to unreliable labels.
Preference pairs, no fine-tuning done yet, tight on memoryORPOFolds fine-tuning and alignment into one pass, saving a stage.
Only thumbs up or down on single answersKTOWorks from plain good or bad labels, no pairs required.
Rewards you can check automatically, like math or codeGRPOScores a group of tries against each other, no critic model.
Maximum quality wanted and cost is no objectPPOThe most powerful, most involved method to run and tune.
No training budget at all, want a quick winBest-of-NGenerate several answers, keep the best. Training-free.

In plain terms

Preference pairTwo answers to the same prompt, one marked better than the other.
Critic modelA helper that estimates how good a partial answer is. PPO needs one; GRPO and DPO skip it.
