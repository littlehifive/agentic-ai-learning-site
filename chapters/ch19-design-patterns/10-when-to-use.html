19.4 Pattern selection: a decision guide

Three questions point you to the right pattern

Choosing comes down to how predictable the task is, how many model calls you can afford in time and cost, and whether quality needs iteration. Start at the top of the list and move down only when the simpler choice clearly fails.

Reach for a fixed workflow when

  • The structure of the task is known in advance.
  • You want predictable cost and easy testing.
  • Steps are sequential: use prompt chaining.
  • Inputs split into clear types: use routing.
  • Subtasks are independent: use parallelization.

Reach for more autonomy when

  • You cannot list the subtasks up front: orchestrator-workers.
  • Quality is critical and needs revision: evaluator-optimizer.
  • The task is open-ended exploration with tools.
  • You accept higher cost and harder debugging.
  • You have shown a simpler pattern is not enough.
