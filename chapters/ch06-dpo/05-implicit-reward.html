6.2 The implicit reward

The model becomes its own judge

PPO needed a separate reward model to score answers. DPO shows that a score is already hiding inside the model itself: compare how likely the model thinks an answer is now against how likely a frozen reference copy thought it was before.

score for an answer = beta × log( policy probability ÷ reference probability )
▲Above the reference: the trained model now finds this answer more likely than the frozen copy did. Positive score, treated as good.
▼Below the reference: the trained model finds it less likely than before. Negative score, treated as bad.
Think of it like grading on a curve against your past self. The reward is not an absolute mark; it is how much more, or less, the model leans toward this answer than the version it started from. Training simply pushes the chosen answer above the rejected one.

In plain terms

PolicyThe model being trained; its answer probabilities shift during training.
ReferenceA frozen copy from the start, kept as the baseline to measure change against.
Implicit rewardA score read off the model itself, so no separate scorer is built.
