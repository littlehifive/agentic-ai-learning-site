6.1 Motivation · the training data

The whole recipe runs on preference pairs

Each training example is one prompt with two answers: a chosen one that people preferred and a rejected one they preferred less. The model never sees a numeric score, only which of the two was better.

Prompt
Explain quantum entanglement to a 10-year-old.
Chosen
▲ Preferred
Imagine two magic coins. Flip one and it lands heads, and the other instantly becomes tails, no matter how far apart they are.
Rejected
▼ Not preferred
Quantum entanglement is a phenomenon where two particles become correlated such that the state of one cannot be described independently.
Think of it like A vs B taste tests, not a scored exam. You do not tell the model the dish earned 8.5 out of 10. You just say this plate beat that plate, again and again.

In plain terms

Chosen answerThe response people preferred for that prompt; the target to move toward.
Rejected answerThe weaker response for the same prompt; the one to move away from.
