6.1 Motivation

The older method works, but it is a lot of machinery

The standard way to align a model, called PPO, keeps four models running at once and wraps them in a delicate reinforcement-learning loop that is famously hard to keep stable. DPO asks a simple question: can we skip the loop and learn straight from preferences?

What PPO needs running

The model being trained, a frozen copy to stay close to, a separate reward model that scores answers, and a value head that predicts future reward. Four models, plus the loop that ties them together.

The key insight behind DPO

The math that PPO optimizes has a clean, exact solution. That lets us rewrite the whole goal as one ordinary supervised loss, which quietly chases the same target without any of the loop.

Think of it like training a chef. PPO hires a separate food critic, sends out dishes, collects scores, and slowly adjusts. DPO just hands the chef matched plates, this one is better than that one, and lets them learn the difference directly.

In plain terms

AlignmentShaping a model so its answers match what people actually prefer.
The reward modelA second model PPO trains to score answers. DPO removes the need for it.
