16.8 Evaluation and failure modes

Two stages, two ways to fail

A RAG answer is only as good as both halves of the pipeline. Errors creep in at retrieval or at generation, and they compound: a perfect writer cannot rescue the wrong passages, and a perfect retriever is wasted if the model ignores them.

It fetched nothing useful

The right passage exists in the corpus but never surfaces, usually from poor chunking or a vocabulary gap between the question and the text.

It drowned in the middle

Stuff too many passages in and the relevant one gets buried. Models attend most to the start and end of a long context and skim the middle.

It ignored the evidence

Even with the right passages on hand, the model can fall back on memory, contradict the context, or cite a source that does not actually support the claim.

Think of it like an open-book exam that still goes wrong. You can grab the wrong chapter, bury the key page under a stack of others, or read the right page and answer from memory anyway. Each failure has a different fix.
