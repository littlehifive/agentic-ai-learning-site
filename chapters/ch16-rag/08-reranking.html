16.5.2 Re-ranking

A careful second read of the shortlist

First-stage search is fast but rough: it scores the question and each passage separately. A reranker then reads the question and each top candidate together, side by side, and re-scores them for true relevance, keeping only the best.

Stage 1

Cast a wide net

Fast retrieval pulls in roughly the top 20 to 100 candidate passages. Generous, not yet precise.

→rerank the shortlist

Stage 2

Read each one closely

A cross-encoder weighs the question against each candidate jointly, scoring relevance far more accurately.

→keep the top few

Stage 3

Hand over the best

Only the top few survivors go into the prompt, so the model reasons over signal, not noise.

Think of it like sorting a stack of resumes. A quick keyword skim narrows a thousand down to twenty. Then you actually read those twenty against the job, side by side, to pick the real top five. Slow per item, but you only do it on the shortlist.

In plain terms

Cross-encoderA model that reads the question and one passage together, giving a sharp relevance score.
RerankingRe-scoring a small candidate set for precision, after fast retrieval narrows the field.
