16.4 Chunking strategies

Documents are sliced into bite-sized passages

You cannot search or hand over a whole report at once, so each document is split into chunks: passages small enough to fit the search step, yet large enough to make sense on their own.

Think of it like cutting a textbook into index cards. Too big and a card buries the answer in noise; too small and it loses the context that made the fact meaningful. A slight overlap between cards keeps a sentence from being torn in half at the seam.

In plain terms

ChunkA single passage, often a few hundred words, stored and retrieved as one unit.
OverlapRepeating a little text across neighbouring chunks so a thought is not split at a boundary.
Chunk sizeThe biggest lever in RAG design: small for precise facts, larger for summaries.
