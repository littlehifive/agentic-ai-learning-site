16.5.1 Query transformation

Fix the question before you search

A raw question is often short, vague, or worded nothing like the documents. Rewriting it first, before retrieval, closes that gap and surfaces passages a literal search would miss.

Draft a fake answer

Have the model sketch a plausible answer, then search with that. It reads like the real documents, so it matches them better.

Known as HyDE: hypothetical document embeddings.

Step back

For a narrow question, also ask a broader one, retrieve for both, and combine. The general context fills in what the specific search alone would miss.

"Boiling point of ethanol at 2 atm?" plus "What affects boiling points?"

Ask it many ways

Generate several rephrasings of the question, search for each, and pool the results. Different wordings reach different passages.

Multi-query retrieval; RAG-Fusion blends the lists with RRF.

Think of it like rephrasing a clumsy search before you hit enter. A librarian who hears a half-formed question will quietly translate it into the terms the catalogue actually uses, then look that up instead.

In plain terms

Query transformationRewriting or expanding the question to retrieve better matches.
HyDESearching with a generated draft answer instead of the bare question.
