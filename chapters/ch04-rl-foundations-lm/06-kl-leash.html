4.3 The KL constraint

The KL penalty is a leash on how far the model can wander

Chasing a reward with no limit is dangerous. The model can find weird outputs that score high but read as nonsense: it learns to game the judge rather than answer well. The KL penalty adds a cost for drifting away from the original fine-tuned model, so each update stays close to known-good behavior.

Without the leashReward hacking

The model exploits quirks in the scorer: padding, flattery, repeated phrases that happen to score high. The reward goes up; the writing falls apart.

With the leashStable improvement

Each step may move only a little from the starting model. The model still climbs the reward, but it cannot bolt off into degenerate, off-topic text.

Think of it like a dog on a leash exploring a park. It can roam and sniff out better paths, but it cannot sprint into traffic. The leash length is a dial: too tight and it never explores, too loose and it runs off.

In plain terms

Reference modelThe fixed starting point (the fine-tuned model) that the leash is anchored to.
KL divergenceA measure of how far the new model's word choices have drifted from the reference. The penalty grows with that distance.
Reward hackingScoring high by exploiting the judge instead of genuinely answering better. The leash is the main defense.
