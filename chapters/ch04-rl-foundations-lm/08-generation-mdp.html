4.2 Text generation as a decision process

Writing one token at a time is a string of decisions

RL fits language models because writing a response is a sequence of choices. At each step the model reads everything so far (the state), picks the next token (the action), and the text grows by one (the transition). When the answer is done, a judge scores it (the reward). The model already is the decision-maker: its next-token probabilities are the policy.

The capital of France is → Paris … end: score the whole answer
State: the prompt plus every token written so far Action: choose the next token, gold above Reward: usually given once, at the end
Think of it like a writer filling a page word by word. Each word depends on all the words before it, the page only ever grows (you never un-write a word), and the grade arrives when the piece is finished. Training adjusts the writer's instincts so finished pieces score higher.
