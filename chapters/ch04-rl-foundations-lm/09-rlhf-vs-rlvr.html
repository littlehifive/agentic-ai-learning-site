4.1 Choosing a paradigm

RLHF or RLVR? It comes down to who can grade

Click a tab to compare

Use it for

Subjective, human-judged goals

Helpfulness, tone, safety, following instructions. Anything where "better" is a matter of human taste, not a single correct answer.

Where reward comes from

A learned reward model

People compare answers; a reward model learns to predict those preferences and scores new answers.

Watch out for

Reward hacking

The learned judge can be gamed, so the KL leash and good preference data matter a lot.

Use it for

Checkable, correct-or-not goals

Math, code, logic, reasoning. Anything where a machine can confirm the answer is right or the tests pass.

Where reward comes from

An automatic verifier

No human judge and no learned reward model: a checker grades each attempt, so it is cheap and hard to fake.

Watch out for

No verifier, no RLVR

It only works where outcomes are truly verifiable. For open-ended writing you fall back to preferences.

Same training engine, different judge. Use RLHF when only a human can say which answer is better; use RLVR when a program can. Real systems often do both: align with preferences, then sharpen skills with verifiers.

In plain terms

Shared machineryBoth use a policy, a reward signal, a KL leash, and policy-gradient training (PPO or GRPO).
