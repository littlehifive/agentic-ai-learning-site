20.2.3 and 20.7 What makes a benchmark trustworthy

A good agentic benchmark is realistic, verifiable, and hard to game

The benchmarks worth trusting share three traits. Miss any one and the leaderboard stops meaning anything: scores rise while real ability does not.

Realistic

The tasks mirror real work, real websites, real repositories, real desktops, so a high score predicts real usefulness, not just lab-bench skill.

Verifiable

There is an objective way to check success, run the tests, inspect the final state. Two labs running it get the same answer, so results are reproducible.

Hard to game

The agent cannot earn a high score without truly doing the task. No peeking at the answer, no shortcut that fakes the result.

Think of it like a good driving test. It happens on real roads (realistic), the examiner checks whether you actually parked between the lines (verifiable), and you cannot pass by describing how you would drive (hard to game).

In plain terms

Human baselineHow well people do, collected as the bar that tells you what a score really means.
ContaminationWhen answers leak into training data; a constant threat to keeping a benchmark honest.
