29.1 Summary, the recurring lesson

The biggest lesson: it is a systems problem, not a prompting trick

A clever prompt or a good loss function is never the whole story. Real alignment means juggling several models at once, spreading the work across hundreds of machines, recovering from failures, and watching for the model gaming its own reward, all at the same time. The wins come from the system around the model, not the model alone.

Think of it like a restaurant, not a recipe. A great dish needs a kitchen, a supply chain, timing, and staff who recover when an order goes wrong. The recipe matters, but it is the smallest part of serving dinner every night.

In plain terms

Systems problemSuccess depends on many moving parts working together, not on one clever component.
Reward hackingWhen a model scores well on the metric but does the wrong thing in practice. A system has to catch this.
Fault toleranceKeeping a long, expensive training or agent run going even when individual machines fail.
